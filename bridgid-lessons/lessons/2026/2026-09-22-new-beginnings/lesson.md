**New Beginnings**

When Iaroslav arrived in Tbilisi, he knew almost no Georgian and a little English. His first month was **non-stop**: new flat, work, new city, **sorting out** his visa and banking all at once. It felt like **sink or swim** from day one. Nobody was going to slow down and wait for him to catch up. At work, everyone assumed he'd already **figured out** the local systems, and honestly, he felt completely **out of his depth.**

The first week was a **steep learning curve**. Slowly, things changed. After a few months he could **get by** in Georgian at the market, order coffee without switching to Russian or English, and he'd finally started **getting the hang of** which co-working spots actually had decent wifi and which crowd hung where, something that had baffled him at first. He still wasn't sure he was **living up to his own expectations**. But looking back, he realised he'd **risen to the challenge** more than he gave himself credit for, and somewhere along the way, Tbilisi had started to feel like home.

**Discuss**

1. When you think about your English, would you say you're finally **getting the hang of it**, or does it still feel like a **steep learning curve**? Why?  
2. Would you rather your week be **non-stop and exciting**, or slow and predictable? Which matches your actual week?  
3. Tell me about a time you **rose to the challenge** and did better than expected.

**Match the situation to one of the phrases**

*be out of your depth*  
*get by*  
*get the hang of (something)*  
*rise to the challenge*  
*figure out / work out*  
   
**get the hang of (something):** I've used this app for three months and now I understand every feature.

**get by:** I don't have much money this month, but I have enough to manage.

**be out of your depth:** I moved somewhere I don't speak the language and felt completely lost in my first meeting.

**figure out / work out:** The office was chaotic, but by Friday we'd solved everything.

**rise to the challenge:** Everyone said the new employee wouldn't manage the presentation, but she did brilliantly and even impressed the board
