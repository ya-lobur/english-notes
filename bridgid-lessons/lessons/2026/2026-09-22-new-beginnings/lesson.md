# Lesson: New beginnings

Материалы урока: [Grammar](grammar.md) · [Vocabulary](vocabulary.md) · [Idioms and phrasal verbs](idioms.md)

## Коротко об уроке

- Главная тема: адаптация после переезда и повторение выражений о трудностях и обучении.
- Что уже получается: выражения встроены в связный персонализированный текст, а не даны изолированно.
- Главный приоритет: точный выбор времени в истории и различие `figure out`, `work out`, `sort out`.
- Авторство пометок: текст о Iaroslav и упражнения подготовлены преподавателем; жирным отмечены целевые выражения.

## Исправления и уточнения

| Original                                            | Better English                                | Тип         | Объяснение                                                                                                                                        |
|-----------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|-------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `new flat, work, new city`                          | `a new flat, a new job, a new city`           | naturalness | В перечислении конкретных новых обстоятельств исчисляемым существительным нужны артикли; `work` меняется на `job`, если имеется в виду должность. |
| `he'd finally started getting the hang of which...` | `he'd finally started to figure out which...` | naturalness | `Get the hang of` обычно сочетается с навыком/действием; перед embedded question естественнее `figure out`.                                       |
| `which crowd hung where`                            | возможно, разговорно                          | register    | Естественная сжатая разговорная формулировка о социальных группах и местах.                                                                       |

## Как это слышит носитель

- `Get by in Georgian` означает владеть языком на минимально достаточном бытовом уровне.
- `More than he gave himself credit for` — признание, что человек недооценивал собственный успех.
- В тексте `sink or swim` подчёркивает отсутствие времени на постепенную адаптацию.

## Active recall

1. Исправь: `I moved to new city and rented new flat.`
2. Выбери: `get by` или `get the hang of` для базового общения на рынке.
3. Перефразируй: `He underestimated how well he had coped.`
4. Соотнеси пять ситуаций с выражениями из исходного упражнения.

## Production practice

1. Расскажи о первых неделях после переезда, используя шесть выражений урока.
2. Опиши ситуацию, в которой сначала был `out of your depth`, но затем `rose to the challenge`.
3. Сравни навык, который ты уже `got the hang of`, с навыком, где пока только `get by`.

## Original notes — verbatim

**22 September 2026**

**Biodegradable / compostable**

**New Beginnings**

When Iaroslav arrived in Tbilisi, he knew almost no Georgian and a little English. His first month was **non-stop**: new
flat, work, new city, **sorting out** his visa and banking all at once. It felt like **sink or swim** from day one.
Nobody was going to slow down and wait for him to catch up. At work, everyone assumed he'd already **figured out** the
local systems, and honestly, he felt completely **out of his depth.**

The first week was a **steep learning curve**. Slowly, things changed. After a few months he could **get by** in
Georgian at the market, order coffee without switching to Russian or English, and he'd finally started **getting the
hang of** which co-working spots actually had decent wifi and which crowd hung where, something that had baffled him at
first. He still wasn't sure he was **living up to his own expectations**. But looking back, he realised he'd **risen to
the challenge** more than he gave himself credit for, and somewhere along the way, Tbilisi had started to feel like
home.

**Discuss**

1. When you think about your English, would you say you're finally **getting the hang of it**, or does it still feel
   like a **steep learning curve**? Why?
2. Would you rather your week be **non-stop and exciting**, or slow and predictable? Which matches your actual week?
3. Tell me about a time you **rose to the challenge** and did better than expected.

**Match the situation to one of the phrases**

*be out of your depth*  
*get by*  
*get the hang of (something)*  
*rise to the challenge*  
*figure out / work out*

**get the hang of (something):** I've used this app for three months and now I understand every feature.

**get by:** I don't have much money this month, but I have enough to manage.

**be out of your depth:** I moved somewhere I don't speak the language and felt completely lost in my first meeting.

**figure out / work out:** The office was chaotic, but by Friday we'd solved everything.

**rise to the challenge:** Everyone said the new employee wouldn't manage the presentation, but she did brilliantly and
even impressed the board

**Discuss**

**out x2 of by**

* If you suddenly had to compete on a cooking show tomorrow, would you be totally **out of your depth**, or would you
  somehow **get by**?
* What's an everyday or completely useless skill you've actually **got the hang of:**  juggling, whistling, parallel
  parking, impressions?
* Imagine your pet or a small child had to survive a normal day in your job. How fast do you think they'd **figure**
  **out** what to do?
* What's something in your life right now that feels **non-stop**.
