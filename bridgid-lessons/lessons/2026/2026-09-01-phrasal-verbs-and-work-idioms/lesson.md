# Lesson: Phrasal verbs review and work idioms

Материалы урока: [Grammar](grammar.md) · [Vocabulary](vocabulary.md) · [Idioms and phrasal verbs](idioms.md)

## Коротко об уроке

- Главные темы: повторение advanced phrasal verbs и новый набор выражений для работы и презентаций.
- Что уже получается: преобразования предложений в основном используют правильные phrasal verbs и формы времени.
- Главный приоритет: устойчивые дополнения (`push back on`, `opt out of`) и естественная последовательность времён.
- Авторство пометок: жирные формы в transformations выглядят как ответы преподавателя.

## Исправления и уточнения

| Original                                  | Better English                              | Тип         | Объяснение                                                                                                     |
|-------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|-------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `bear with someone who is annoying`       | `put up with someone who is annoying`       | meaning     | `Bear with me` — просьба о терпении на короткое время; `put up with` — терпеть неприятного человека/поведение. |
| `he’d had to do his homework`             | `he’d have to do his homework`              | grammar     | После `He knew that if he wanted...` ожидается `would have to`; past perfect здесь меняет смысл.               |
| `all we need is the green light from you` | без изменений как прямая речь               | style       | Естественно в цитате; в косвенной речи нужно согласовать лицо и время.                                         |
| `a plan up your sleeve`                   | `a trick / an idea / a plan up your sleeve` | naturalness | Все варианты возможны; классическая форма — `have a trick up your sleeve`.                                     |

## Как это слышит носитель

- `Do your homework` на работе означает подготовиться и изучить вопрос, а не школьное домашнее задание.
- `Green light` — официальное разрешение; просто общее одобрение может быть слабее.
- `Go with the flow` может звучать как гибкость или как отсутствие инициативы — зависит от контекста.

**Sentence Transformation**

Complete the second sentence so it means the same as the first using the phrasal verbs.

1. Please be patient with me while I sort this out.  
   Please **bear with** me while I sort this out.
2. Her leadership caused major changes in the company.  
   Her leadership **brought about** major changes in the company.
3. We faced a lot of unexpected problems during the launch.  
   We **came up against** a lot of unexpected problems during the launch.
4. The author added more detail to the characters over several months.  
   The author spent months **fleshing out** the characters.
5. They negotiated the contract details until both sides were happy.  
   They **hammered out** the contract details until both sides were happy.
6. When I think about my childhood, I feel very lucky.  
   **Looking back on** my childhood, I feel very lucky.
7. I need time to think carefully about the job offer.  
   I need time to **mull over** the job offer.
8. He chose not to take part in the project because he was too busy.  
   He **opted out** of the project because he was too busy.
9. I'll make sure your message reaches the manager.  
   I'll **pass on** your message to the manager.
10. The company will gradually stop using the old software.  
    The company will **phase out** the old software.

**Do your homework:** Be well-prepared; research or practice before doing something.  
*Before the interview, she really did her homework and learned all about the company*  
**Green light:** Official permission to start something.  
*The boss gave us the green light to start the new project*  
**Hit the ground running**: To start a new job or task quickly and with energy, without needing much training or
preparation.  
*She really hit the ground running in her new role—by the end of the first week, she had already led two meetings and
solved a key issue.*  
**At your fingertips:** Easily available or accessible.  
*With smartphones, so much information is at your fingertips these days.*  
**The personal touch:** A customized or human approach that makes something feel special.  
*The hotel added the personal touch by leaving handwritten notes in the rooms*  
**To come up**: To arise or occur, especially unexpectedly.  
*A problem or opportunity that appears during work or a project.*  
*A last-minute issue came up during the client meeting, so we had to delay the presentation.*  
**Go with the flow:** Adapt easily; not resist change or challenges.  
*When traveling, it’s best to go with the flow if your plans change unexpectedly.*  
**Get carried away:** Become overly enthusiastic or excited, often losing control.  
*They got carried away during the debate and started shouting.*  
**Up your sleeve:** To have a secret plan or idea that you’re saving for later.  
*Don’t worry, he has a few tricks up his sleeve for the presentation*  
**Mind goes blank:** Forget everything suddenly, especially under pressure.  
*During the exam, my mind went blank, and I forgot everything I studied.*  
**Be ahead of the game:** To be more prepared or advanced than others.  
*By learning AI tools early, she’s ahead of the game in her career.*  
**To move forward**: To make progress or continue with the next step in a process.  
*Now that we’ve received management approval, we can move forward with the product launch.*  
**To pay off**: To result in success or a positive outcome after effort or investment.  
*All those late nights working on the proposal really paid off when we won the contract.*

Iaroslav’s Big DAY

It was the day of the big presentation, and Iaroslav was feeling a mix of excitement and nerves. He had known that if he
wanted his presentation to be a success, he’d had to **do his homework** by researching the latest market trends and
gathering data to support his ideas. With the latest information **at his fingertips**, he felt prepared to answer any
questions that might **come up**

Iaroslav smiled, ready to engage with his colleagues. He always liked to **give** his presentations **​the personal
touch**, tailoring his message to meet the audience's needs.

He finished by telling his manager that all we need is ​the **green light** from you to get started. Despite being so
prepared, he had to remind himself to **go with the flow** and to stay calm. In the past, he often got **carried**
**away** in his excitement over his ideas.

By the end of the meeting, the team was energized and ready to ​move forward. The manager was impressed and gave the
approval they were hoping for. Iaroslav and his team were ready to **hit the ground running**, excited to begin the
project with focus and energy.  
Iaroslav left the room feeling a sense of achievement, knowing that his hard work had **paid off**
