**Communication Idioms** 

**Word of mouth:** information spread by people talking to each other, not through official channels  
I found out about the job opening through word of mouth, a friend mentioned it at dinner.  
**On the tip of your tongue:** you know something but can't quite recall it in the moment  
His name is on the tip of my tongue, I've met him three times\!  
**Like talking to a brick wall:** trying to communicate with someone who won't listen or respond  
I kept explaining the deadline to him, but it was like talking to a brick wall.  
**A mile a minute:** speaking very fast, making it hard to follow  
The client talks a mile a minute in meetings — I always ask her to slow down.  
**Off the top of your head:** from memory, without checking or thinking too hard. Off the top of my head, I'd say we have around 200 employees, but don't quote me on that.  
**Speak your mind:** say exactly what you think, directly and honestly  
He finally spoke his mind in the meeting and told the manager the plan wasn't working.  
**Beat around the bush:** avoid getting to the point; talk around a topic instead of addressing it directly  
Stop beating around the bush, just tell me what the problem is.  
**Get your point across:** to successfully make someone understand what you mean  
The client didn't speak much English, but she used visuals to get her point across.  
**On the same page:** when everyone has the same understanding or information  
Before we start the project, let's make sure we're all on the same page about the deadlines  
**Read between the lines:** to understand the hidden or implied meaning behind what someone says  
She said she was 'fine' with the decision, but if you read between the lines, she clearly wasn't happy.
