## Исправления и уточнения

| Original                               | Better English                              | Тип         | Объяснение                                                                                          |
|----------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|-------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `I preferred the mountains to the sea` | `I preferred...` / `I prefer...`            | meaning     | Past Simple относится к прошлому выбору; для нынешнего общего предпочтения нужен Present Simple.    |
| `more interested in`                   | `I’m more interested in AI than in crypto.` | grammar     | `Interested` описывает чувство человека; модель сравнения — `more ... than ...`.                    |
| `implement the news`                   | `apply the technology/the idea`             | naturalness | Внедряют технологию, решение или идею, но не саму новость.                                          |
| `Doesn’t work well`                    | `It doesn’t work well.`                     | grammar     | Как короткая реакция фрагмент естественен; самостоятельному полному предложению нужно подлежащее.   |
| `Points out / highlights / flags`      | зависит от цели                             | meaning     | `Point out` сообщает факт, `highlight` подчёркивает важность, `flag` обозначает требующее внимания. |
| `To be under the hood`                 | `under the hood`                            | naturalness | Устойчивое выражение не требует `to be` как части словарной формы.                                  |

## Как это слышит носитель

- `Flag` часто создаёт ожидание проблемы или риска; `highlight` лишь делает что-то заметным.
- `On a rollercoaster` — разговорный образ; в аналитическом тексте точнее `highly volatile`.
- `It’s good all round` естественно, особенно в BrE; AmE чаще использует `all-around` перед существительным.
- `Newbie` — дружелюбное неформальное слово; в формальном контексте точнее `someone with no prior knowledge`.

## Pronunciation

- `interested` обычно /ˈɪntrəstɪd/; окончание `-ed` здесь /ɪd/.
- В `volatility` главное ударение падает на `-til-`: /ˌvɒləˈtɪləti/ или /ˌvɑːləˈtɪləti/.

## Active recall

1. Исправь: `I prefer working from home than working in the office.`
2. Выбери: `I’m interesting / interested in practical AI tools.`
3. Вставь `point out`, `highlight` или `flag`: `The system ___ three suspicious payments.`
4. Преобразуй: `We changed one line of code.` → cleft sentence с `All`.
5. Перефразируй с `outright`: `They paid the full price without a loan.`

## Production practice

1. Объясни новичку cryptocurrency простыми словами.
2. Расскажи о функции, которую команда `rolled out`: что происходило `under the hood` и какие проблемы пришлось
   `iron out`.
3. Составь три cleft sentences о рабочем проекте: с `What`, `It was ... that` и `The reason ... was`.

**1\. To roll out** (phrasal verb) to release or launch something to users  
*We rolled out the new wallet feature to a small group of users first.*

*The new trading platform goes live next Monday.*

**Cleft Sentences**

A cleft sentence takes information that could be expressed in one simple clause and splits it into two clauses, in order
to emphasise a particular part of the sentence.

**Plain sentence:** The new regulation caused the delay.  
**Cleft sentence:** It was the new regulation that caused the delay.

Both sentences give the same information, but the cleft version puts extra focus on the new regulation.

1. **What-clefts**

*Structure: What \+ clause \+ be \+ emphasised information*  
Used to highlight an action, result, or need. Very common when explaining problems or priorities.

* What caused the crash was a coding error.  
  What we need is clearer documentation.

The emphasised part can also come first:

* A coding error was what caused the crash.

2. **Cleft Sentences with the thing / the person / the place... etc.**

*Structure: The \+ noun \+ clause \+ be \+ emphasised information*  
This is a variation of the what-cleft, where instead of **what**, you use a specific noun (**the thing, the person, the
place, the moment, the reason**) to introduce the emphasised part. It's slightly more natural and specific-sounding than
**what**, especially in spoken English.

The thing I love about my job is the flexibility.  
(= What I love about my job is the flexibility.)  
The person who inspires me most is my old manager.  
The place I feel most creative is my home office.  
The moment everything changed was when we launched the app.  
The reason I moved into fintech was the pace of innovation.

3. **All-clefts**

*Structure: All \+ clause \+ be \+ emphasised information*  
Used to minimise or simplify. Often used to say something was easier or smaller than expected.  
**We changed one line of code**   
All we did was change one line of code.  
All it took was one bad headline to trigger the sell-off.  
**We are changing the code**   
All we are doing is changing the code   
What we are doing is changing the code

(if the auxiliary in the wh-clause is a form of *did/do/does* (including *have to do*, *need to do*, *want to do*), the
complement is bare infinitive. If the auxiliary is continuous (*am/is/are doing*), the complement is \-ing, because
you're essentially equating two gerund-like ideas rather than using **do** as a dummy verb for a base-form action)

4. **The reason (why) clefts**

*Structure: The reason (why) \+ clause \+ be \+ explanation*  
Useful for giving causes and explanations clearly.

The reason the launch was delayed was a last-minute security issue.

**\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_**

5. **It-clefts**

*Structure: It \+ be \+ emphasised information \+ that/who \+ rest of sentence*  
Used to highlight a specific person, thing, time, or reason. More formal \- common in written texts

It was the CEO who made the final decision.  
It wasn't the price that put investors off, it was the volatility.
