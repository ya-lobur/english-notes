**Give me three numbers 1-7**

**in the loop**  
to be kept informed and involved in updates  
*Let’s keep Sarah **in the loop** so she knows about all the changes to the launch schedule.*  
**crunch the numbers**  
to process large amounts of data quickly  
*Before we decide on the budget, we need to **crunch the numbers** to see if it’s financially viable.*  
**a learning curve**  
the period it takes to learn new technology  
*Switching to the new design software has been **a steep learning curve**, but I’m getting the hang of it.*  
**game changer**  
something that changes the status quo  
*The introduction of 5G was a **game changer** for mobile connectivity*  
**On the same wavelength**  
to understand each other well; to be in agreement  
*The design team and the developers are finally on the same wavelength about the app’s interface.*  
***state of the art***  
*the most advanced and up-to-date technology available*  
*Our new lab is equipped with **state-of-the-art** robotics technology.*  
***pull the plug***  
*to terminate or abandon a project*  
*After months of delays and budget overruns, the company finally **pulled the plug** on the project.*  
***open Pandora’s box***  
*something that could cause unexpected problems*  
*Introducing that policy might **open Pandora’s box** of legal challenges.*  
***tick all the boxes***  
*to meet all the necessary requirements or expectations*  
*This new phone ticks all the boxes – it’s fast, affordable, and has a great camera.*  
***think outside the box***  
*to think creatively or in an unconventional way*  
*If we want to solve this software issue, we’ll need to think outside the box.*

1. How do you **stay in the loop** about the latest tech advancements?  
2. Have you ever had to use a new technology or software where the **learning curve was really steep**?  
3. Can you give an example of an AI technology that has been a **game changer** in your industry/workplace?  
4. What are some reasons a company might decide to **pull the plug** on a tech project mid-development?  
5. When was the last time you had to **crunch the numbers** before making a decision? What was it about?  
6. Have you ever worked on a team where people **weren’t on the same wavelength**?  
7. Do you think it’s always worth paying more for **state-of-the-art** technology, or is it better to wait until prices drop? Why?

**LINKERS**

| Contrast linking words Although / even though / though \+ subject \+ verb:  followed by a full clause, can start or sit mid-sentence. Although I enjoy new tech, I don't buy every new release. Whereas / while \+ subject \+ verb: links two contrasting ideas within one sentence, often comparing two people or situations. I love trying new gadgets, whereas my partner sticks with what he knows. Despite / in spite of \+ noun or \-ing form (never a full clause):  Despite the price, I bought it. In spite of struggling with the setup, I got there eventually. However, / nevertheless / on the other hand: linking adverbs, start a new sentence (or follow a semicolon), followed by a comma. The phone is expensive. However, it's worth every penny. |
| :---- |
| **Clauses of purpose To / in order to / so as to \+ infinitive:** all three explain why someone does something and mean the same thing, but in order to and so as to sound slightly more formal. I upgraded my laptop to work faster. I upgraded my laptop in order to work faster. **Negative form: in order not to / so as not to \+ infinitive:** never just "not to" in formal writing. I switched off notifications so as not to get distracted. **So that \+ subject \+ can/could/will/would:** used when the subject of the purpose clause is different from the main clause, or a modal is needed. I bought a bigger screen so that the whole family could watch together. **For \+ noun:** used when the purpose itself is a thing rather than an action. I bought a case for protection. |
